Ask how many creators there are and you will get very different answers. One estimate for the United States alone runs from 30 million to 85 million people. Another puts the worldwide number around 300 million. That is a wide spread, and it is not because anyone is careless. The answer depends on what you count as a creator. This post walks through the numbers that get quoted and what they can and cannot tell you.

## Why the number is hard to pin down

There is no official headcount of creators. Estimates come from different studies, and each one draws the line in its own place. Some measures cover everyone who creates and publishes. Others cover only people who earn money from it.

A report on the creator economy written by Richard Florida and commissioned by Meta uses a broad definition. It defines creators as people who use digital technology to make and publish unique creative content that audiences can access and respond to. A photographer sharing work, a musician posting songs, a writer with a newsletter, and a video maker all fit that definition.

With a broad definition, the count gets large quickly. With a narrow one, it shrinks.

## The ranges that get quoted

The Meta article summarizing the report gives several figures, each from a different source and each measuring something different.

For the United States, the report cites a range of 30 to 85 million creators, based on data and studies it reviewed. For the world, the article cites an estimate of 300 million creators from Edelman Data & Intelligence, from a global study published in August 2022.

:::note Worth knowing
Meta's summary of the Richard Florida report gives a United States estimate of 30 to 85 million creators and a global estimate of 300 million, attributed to Edelman Data & Intelligence (August 2022).
:::

The gap between 30 million and 85 million is large. The article does not explain the methods behind each number, so treat these as estimates, not exact totals.

## Counting people who earn versus people who create

A second kind of number counts only those who earn money. The same article cites an estimate of as many as 17 million creators earning from digital content as of 2017, attributed to a Re:Create report from February 2019.

That figure is far smaller than the larger counts, which shows that counting people who earn money gives a different answer than counting everyone who creates. A single headline number can blend the two, so check what it measures.

The report also mentions the broader creative class, which it says includes up to 60 million Americans, or more than 40 percent of the workforce. That is a different concept from creators who publish online, and it is easy to confuse the two.

:::note Worth knowing
The Meta article attributes an estimate of as many as 17 million creators earning from digital content as of 2017 to a Re:Create report published in February 2019.
:::

## What a big number does not tell you

A national or global estimate says little about your own area. The figures above describe the United States and the world. They do not say how many creators live in Fort Wayne, and the sources used here do not give a local count, so this post does not offer one.

Big numbers also hide how different creators are. A podcaster, an illustrator, a developer, and a filmmaker have different needs, tools, and customers. The count tells you the field is large. It does not tell you who is nearby or who is looking for a collaborator.

## Finding creators near you

That is where a local directory is useful. Creator Space is a creative community directory for Fort Wayne. Its site lists categories such as photographers, videographers, musicians, designers, writers, developers, producers, animators, filmmakers, podcasters, illustrators, and makers. Joining the database is free, and the site describes the roster as open.

Beyond the directory, the site has pages to collaborate with other creators, to hire a creator, and to take part in a skills exchange. It also describes free monthly meetups for creators of all kinds. If you have been working alone, those meetups can be an easy way to meet people in person, and the site is where upcoming events are announced.

## What to take away

No one knows the exact number of creators, and estimates depend on the definition. In the sources used here, the numbers range from 17 million creators earning from digital content in 2017, to 30 to 85 million creators in the United States, to about 300 million worldwide. All of them say the same thing: a lot of people make creative work. The practical question is not how many creators exist. It is whether you can find the few who fit your project, or the few who can help you with yours.
