Headlines about the creator economy often focus on big numbers and famous names. If you are a photographer, musician, designer, writer or video maker in your own city, those headlines can feel far from your day. This post looks at what the numbers actually say, and why they point toward something close to home: the people around you.

## What the creator economy is

The creator economy describes people who make content and earn money from it, along with the platforms, brands and audiences that connect to them. A creator can be a video maker, a podcaster, a writer, an illustrator, a musician or anyone who builds an audience around work they make.

Goldman Sachs Research published an analysis of the field in April 2023. It estimated about 50 million creators worldwide. It also estimated that the total addressable market could roughly double, from $250 billion at that time to $480 billion by 2027.

:::note Worth knowing
Goldman Sachs Research expected the number of creators to grow at a compound annual rate of 10 to 20 percent over the five years after its 2023 report.
:::

## What the numbers say about income

The same research reports that about 4 percent of creators earn more than $100,000 a year. Goldman expected that share to stay steady as the ecosystem grows. In plain terms, a small group earns a high income, and most creators earn less than that.

That does not make the work less valuable. It does mean that for most people, creative work is built from several smaller pieces over time, not from one large income source.

## Where creators earn their money

Goldman Sachs describes three main routes. Creators earn through brand deals, through a share of advertising revenue from the platforms that host their work, and through direct payments from followers such as subscriptions and donations. According to survey data cited in the research, brand deals are the largest source, at about 70 percent.

Each of these routes depends on being found, trusted and hired. A brand deal usually starts with someone who has seen your work or heard about you. Direct support from followers starts with people who care about what you make. Platform income depends on audience size.

:::note Worth knowing
Brand deals were the largest source of creator income in the survey data Goldman Sachs cited, at about 70 percent.
:::

## Why this points toward local connections

If the largest income source starts with someone knowing your work, then being known matters. Online platforms reach many people, but they also hold a very large field of creators. A local scene gives you a smaller field where people can meet you in person, see your work in context and remember your name.

Local connections also help in ways the numbers do not measure. A photographer who knows a videographer can propose a joint project. A musician who knows a designer can get cover art made. A writer who meets a developer can find a person to help build a site. These are practical exchanges between neighbors, and they do not depend on the platforms that Goldman Sachs says will lead growth.

## What the figures do not tell you

The Goldman Sachs research is a market estimate from April 2023. It describes creators worldwide, so it does not say how any one city's creators are doing. It also does not say how a particular photographer, musician or writer should spend their time.

What it does show is the shape of the field. There are about 50 million creators, a small share earn a high income, and brand deals are the largest source of income in the survey data it cites. A creator in any city can read those figures and ask a practical question: who knows my work, and who could hire or work with me?

## What to take from the numbers

The creator economy is large, and Goldman Sachs expected it to keep growing. The same research shows that a small share of creators earn a high income, and that being known by the people who hire and follow you is central to how creators earn.

For a local creative person, that means the people nearby are worth the effort. Meeting them, sharing work with them and learning from them are steps you can take now, and they do not depend on any platform. The more creative people in a city know each other, the more work, ideas and support move between them.
